What to Avoid
Equally important as what to do.
🚫
ULIPs & Endowment Plans
These mix insurance and investment — badly. Charges eat 15–25% of your money in year 1. Buy term insurance separately (you don't need it yet at 26 with no dependents) and invest in mutual funds. Never combine the two.
🚫
Wedding money in equity
Equity can fall 30% in 3 months. If your wedding is in 2 years, that money must be in FD or RD. Always. No exceptions. You cannot afford to delay the wedding because the market crashed.
🚫
Crypto, F&O, Stock Tips
90%+ of retail F&O traders lose money — this is a verified SEBI statistic. Crypto is speculation, not investment. Stock tips are often paid promotions. None of these belong in a beginner's plan.
🚫
Too many funds
The biggest mistake beginners make: opening 6–8 SIPs thinking more = better diversification. It doesn't. Two well-chosen funds (Nifty 50 + Flexi-cap) beat a cluttered portfolio of 8 funds. More funds = more confusion, not more returns.
🚫
Selling SIPs when markets fall
Every market fall feels like "this time is different." It never is. Indian markets recovered from COVID (2020), demonetisation (2016), the 2008 global crash. Your job: do nothing. Keep the SIP. This patience is worth lakhs.
🚫
NBFC FDs for short-term money
Shriram, Bajaj Finance FDs offer 8–9% interest but are not as safe as bank FDs. DICGC insurance covers bank FDs up to ₹5L per bank. For your emergency and wedding fund, use only nationalised or large private banks.

🏛️ Government Schemes — EPF, NPS, PPF

EPFO / Umang App
epfindia.gov.in
🏛️ Government
Track your EPF balance, passbook, and employer contributions. Activate your UAN (Universal Account Number) — this is your EPF identity for life, even when you change jobs. Link Aadhaar to UAN to avoid delays in withdrawal. Transfer (don't withdraw) EPF when switching jobs — you lose interest if you withdraw early and pay tax on it.

💡 Key Concepts — Explained in Plain Language
Tap each to expand. Read these once, understand them forever.
📈
SIP — What it is and why it's powerful
▾
A SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) is a fixed monthly amount invested in a mutual fund — like an EMI but for your future self. Every month on a fixed date, the amount is auto-debited and units of the fund are purchased.

Why it works: You buy fewer units when markets are high, more units when markets are low. Over time this "rupee cost averaging" smooths out volatility and gives better returns than trying to time the market. 99% of people who try to time the market fail. SIP removes the need to be clever.

₹10,000/month SIP in Nifty 50 index fund for 20 years at 11% CAGR = approximately ₹80 lakhs. With step-ups, it could be ₹1.2–1.5 crore.
🔄
Direct vs Regular Mutual Fund — The Hidden Cost
▾
Every mutual fund comes in two versions: Regular and Direct.

Regular plan: You invest through a broker or bank. They earn a commission (called "trail commission") of 0.5–1.2% every year from the fund. This is deducted from your returns silently — you never see it as a charge.

Direct plan: You invest directly through the AMC website, Kuvera, Groww, or MF Central. No middleman = no commission = you get 0.5–1.2% more returns every year.

On ₹15,000/month SIP over 20 years, the difference between Direct and Regular is approximately ₹12–18 lakhs. Same fund, same market — just the plan type.
📊
Index Fund vs Active Fund — Why Simple Wins
▾
Index Fund: Copies a market index like Nifty 50 (top 50 Indian companies by size). A computer automatically buys all 50 stocks in the right proportion. No fund manager trying to be clever. Expense ratio: 0.05–0.2%.

Active Fund: A human fund manager picks stocks trying to beat the index. Expense ratio: 0.5–1.5%. Fund manager may be great or may not be — and you don't know in advance.

The uncomfortable truth: Over 10+ years, 80% of active funds underperform their benchmark index after fees. This is a global phenomenon, not just India. The remaining 20% that outperform — you cannot identify them in advance.

For a beginner or even an experienced investor, a Nifty 50 or Nifty 500 index fund is the most reliable long-term choice. Add one flexi-cap fund for active selection. That's it.
💰
FD vs RD vs Liquid Fund — When to Use Which
▾
Fixed Deposit (FD): Lump sum locked for a fixed period. 6.5–7.5% at large banks, 8–9% at small finance banks. Interest is taxable as income. Best for: emergency fund, wedding seed money you already have. DICGC insured up to ₹5L per bank.

Recurring Deposit (RD): Fixed monthly deposit, auto-debited. Same interest as FD. Best for: building a goal fund month by month (like your ₹15k/month wedding RD). Interest is taxable.

Liquid Fund: A type of mutual fund that invests in very short-term government/corporate debt. Returns ~6.5–7.5%, slightly better than savings account. Can be redeemed in 1 day. Best for: parking money you might need quickly, like a 3-month emergency buffer on top of your FD. Safer than equity but slightly less than FD.

Rule: Emergency fund in FD. Wedding money in RD → then FD. Anything you might need within 24 hours in Liquid Fund or savings account.
🛡️
EPF — India's Most Underrated Wealth Builder
▾
Your employer deducts 12% of your basic salary every month for EPF. They also contribute 12% from their side (though 8.33% of that goes into EPS — the pension scheme). The EPF earns 8.15% interest, declared by the government yearly. This is completely tax-free if you withdraw after 5 years of continuous service.

Critical rules:
  • Never withdraw EPF when changing jobs — transfer it using UAN. Withdrawal is taxed and you lose compounding.
  • Activate your UAN on the EPFO portal. Link Aadhaar to it.
  • Check your EPF passbook every 6 months to ensure employer is depositing.
  • After 10 years of contributions you're eligible for a pension (EPS) at age 58.
EPF is essentially 8.15% guaranteed, tax-free return. No investment product in India beats this risk-return ratio for the same safety level. Never underestimate it.
🧮
Old Tax Regime vs New Tax Regime — Which to Pick
▾
India gives you a choice every year: file under the Old Regime (claim deductions, higher slabs) or New Regime (lower slabs, almost no deductions). Since FY 2024-25, the New Regime is the default.

Old Regime benefits you if: You claim HRA (rent deduction), invest ₹1.5L+ under 80C (EPF + ELSS), pay health insurance premium (₹25k deduction under 80D), and have home loan interest.

New Regime benefits you if: You don't have many deductions, or your income is under ₹7L (zero tax under New Regime).

At your salary (₹72k–92k/month = ₹8.6–11L/year): The old regime often wins if you're paying rent, investing in EPF, and doing ELSS. But the exact answer depends on your HRA and deductions — ask your CA every April to run the numbers for both regimes before deciding.

Switching regimes every year is allowed. Calculate both, pick the lower tax. Your CA takes 15 minutes to do this — it could save ₹15,000–40,000 per year.

What To Do When Markets Fall (The Most Important Lesson)
▾
Markets will fall. Not maybe — will. Indian markets have crashed 30–50% multiple times: 2008, 2011, 2015, 2018, 2020 (COVID), 2022. Every time, the media said "this time is different." Every time, markets recovered to new highs within 1–3 years.

What most people do (wrong): Panic → sell at the bottom → miss the recovery → buy back at higher prices. This locks in losses permanently.

What you should do: Nothing. Your SIP continues automatically. You buy more units at cheaper prices. If you have extra cash, it's actually a good time to invest a lump sum.

The only exception: If the money was needed within 3 years, it should never have been in equity to begin with. That's why your wedding money is in FD, not SIP.

Write this on paper and put it somewhere visible: "My SIP runs no matter what happens in the news. I will not check the value more than once a month."
🔑
Compounding — Why Starting at 26 is a Superpower
▾
Compounding means earning returns on your returns. The longer the time, the more explosive the growth. This is not a metaphor — the math is genuinely dramatic.

Example: ₹10,000/month SIP at 11% CAGR:
  • After 10 years: ~₹20.6L invested → grows to ~₹22L (modest)
  • After 20 years: ~₹24L invested → grows to ~₹80L
  • After 30 years: ~₹36L invested → grows to ~₹2.3 crore
The jump from 20 to 30 years is enormous because the last 10 years compound on a much larger base. Starting at 26 vs 36 is literally the difference between ₹2.3Cr and ₹80L for the same monthly investment.

Your biggest financial advantage right now isn't your salary or your savings. It's your age. Every year you delay starting a SIP costs you roughly ₹15–20L in eventual wealth. You've already started — that's the most important thing.
💡
ELSS — Tax Saving + Investment in One
▾
ELSS (Equity Linked Savings Scheme) is a mutual fund that qualifies for Section 80C deduction. Up to ₹1.5L invested per year reduces your taxable income by that amount under the Old Tax Regime.

Why ELSS is better than other 80C options:
  • PPF: 15-year lock-in. ELSS: only 3-year lock-in.
  • NSC/KVP: Fixed ~7% returns. ELSS: equity returns (historically 12–14%).
  • Tax-saving FD: 5-year lock. ELSS: 3-year lock.
How it fits your plan: Your EPF already contributes to 80C. If EPF contribution is less than ₹1.5L/year, top up with ELSS SIP to fill the remaining gap. Discuss with CA in January each year.

An ELSS SIP of ₹5,000/month (₹60k/year) gives you ₹60k in tax deduction AND equity market returns. It's the smartest way to save tax for a salaried professional.
⚖️
Expense Ratio — The Fee That Silently Eats Your Returns
▾
Every mutual fund charges an annual fee called the expense ratio. It's a percentage of your total invested amount, deducted daily from the fund's NAV — you never see it as a separate charge, which is why it's often ignored.

Typical expense ratios:
  • Index Fund (Direct): 0.05–0.2%
  • Active Fund (Direct): 0.5–1.2%
  • Active Fund (Regular): 1.0–2.0%
What this means in rupees: On ₹10L corpus, a 1.5% expense ratio costs ₹15,000/year — every year. A 0.1% index fund costs ₹1,000/year. Over 20 years the difference compounds to lakhs.

Always check the expense ratio before starting a SIP. For your funds: index fund should be under 0.2%, flexi-cap fund should be under 0.8% (Direct plan).
