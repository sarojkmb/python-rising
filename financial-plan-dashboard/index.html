5-Year Timeline

Milestones are sequenced to protect short-term goals first, then increase long-term compounding.

Hands-off
First 30 Days

Organize and automate

Separate ₹3.5L-₹4L as emergency fund. Move ₹1L-₹1.5L into a wedding fund bucket. Confirm current SIP fund names and check whether they are Direct Growth. Set a ₹15k/month wedding RD or liquid fund transfer.

90 Days

Raise SIP from ₹6k to ₹10k

Use one index fund as the core. Keep wedding money separate. Review actual spending after promotion and make sure monthly expenses have not crossed ₹60k-62k.

6 Months

Move SIP toward ₹13k

If the first six months feel manageable, increase SIP. Keep emergency fund untouched. Buy an independent personal health policy if she wants protection outside employer coverage.

1 Year

Reach ₹15k SIP

Target ₹10k in a Nifty 50 or Nifty 500 index fund and ₹5k in a flexi-cap fund. Keep wedding fund on track at ₹15k/month. Do not add stocks yet unless she has a learning-only budget.

2 Years

Wedding fund should be nearly ready

Expected wedding bucket: about ₹4.6L-₹5L depending on returns and starting seed. Keep it safe. Reduce market risk further if the wedding is within 12 months.

3 Years

Complete wedding goal and reset

After wedding spending, rebuild emergency fund if used. Redirect the ₹15k wedding savings into long-term SIP, house down-payment fund, or both.

4 Years

Start house fund seriously

If buying in Bangalore is still 5+ years away, use mostly equity mutual funds. If purchase is closer than 5 years, use a safer mix with FD/debt allocation.

5 Years

Shift to wealth-building mode

Target ₹30k-₹40k/month total long-term investing if income has grown. Keep 6 months emergency fund, personal health cover, EPF, and simple direct mutual fund SIPs.

Do Not Buy

These create complexity before she needs it.

Avoid ULIPs, endowment plans, crypto, F&O, sector funds, stock tips, and putting wedding money in equity. Limit Shriram/NBFC FD exposure because it is not the same safety category as a bank FD.

Market Fall Rule

This rule matters more than fund selection.

If equity mutual funds fall 20-30%, do not sell long-term SIPs. Continue the SIP. Only money needed within 3 years should be outside equity before the fall happens.
